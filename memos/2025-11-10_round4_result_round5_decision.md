## Round 5 decision

Choice: leave 10,000 units unhedged, hedge 30,000 units using the swap at $56, and hedge the remaining 60,000 units using a collar by buying the $45 put and selling the $75 call.

Rationale: I picked the combination hedge because it gives a good balance between safety and still having a chance to benefit if prices go up. Hedging 30,000 units with the swap at $56 helps protect the company if prices fall a lot. This way, we have a guaranteed portion of revenue and are less likely to drop below the level that would cause bankruptcy costs.

For 60,000 units, I chose the collar because it basically gives us a safety net without costing much. The put at $45 sets a floor so we don't get crushed if prices drop really low. The call at $75 helps pay for the put, so the hedge is pretty cheap. The downside is that our upside is limited above $75, but at least we're not paying a big premium like we would with just a put alone.

I left 10,000 units unhedged so that if the price ends up being higher, we can still enjoy some of that increase instead of being fully capped.

Overall, this approach keeps us safe on the downside, doesn't cost much, and still allows some upside if the market does better than expected.

Final decision: 10,000 float, 30,000 at swap rate of $56, and 60,000 collar (put $45 / call $75).
